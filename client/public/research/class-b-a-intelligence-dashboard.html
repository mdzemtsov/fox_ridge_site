Who Is Actually Distressed — Class B+/A Bridge Loan Sellers

The $163B in securitized CRE maturities hitting in 2026 includes a large cohort of B+/A multifamily assets acquired in 2021–2022 at peak pricing with 3-year floating-rate bridge loans. These operators underwrote to 2021 rent levels, expected 5%+ annual rent growth, and financed at <4% floating rates. Today they face: (1) in-place rents 10–20% below their underwritten assumptions; (2) floating rates that have doubled their debt service; (3) rate caps expired. These are the B+/A motivated sellers. FoxRidge targets their basis — entering at real 2026 NOI with defensible underwriting, not their 2021 pro forma. The Dallas Federal Reserve confirmed in March 2026 that Texas banks are modifying B+/A multifamily loans above national average rates. This is a pipeline signal, not a warning sign.

Supply & Demand — Class B+/A Is NOT Being Built

The single most important supply insight for FoxRidge: virtually all new multifamily supply is Class A luxury new construction. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac both confirm this. Class B+/A is not being built at scale in any target market. FoxRidge is buying existing B+/A stock at reset basis, not competing with the supply wave. Sources: Freddie Mac 2025 Outlook PDF (Exhibit 4, directly fetched), Yardi Matrix, Fannie Mae, Cushman & Wakefield, NAHB.

Critical B+/A Supply Asymmetry — The Core Investment Thesis
What Is Being Delivered (2026)
~85% of the 450,000 units delivering in 2026 are Class A luxury new construction. High-rise urban cores, amenity-heavy suburban mid-rise, high-end garden. Average asking rent $2,200+. Financed during the 2021–2022 development boom at peak land cost. This is NOT what FoxRidge buys.
What FoxRidge Buys
Existing Class B+/A assets, 15–35 years old, suburban locations, middle-income renter base. B+/A is <12% of new deliveries nationally — and that small slice is only repositioned/converted product, not ground-up. Class B+/A does not face a supply wave. It faces a price reset opportunity.
The Displacement Effect (Why B+/A Absorbs)
Class A concessions (2–4 months free rent) briefly draw Class B renters UP — the "flight to quality" effect. This is temporary and creates short-term B+/A vacancy. But as Class A concessions burn off (H2 2026–2027), those renters return to B+/A OR permanently reset to the class they can actually sustain. B+/A vacancy recovers first.

Full Risk Register — All GO NOW Markets
TX Property Tax Reassessment
Texas resets assessed value to transaction price on sale. A $30M acquisition on property assessed at $18M triggers ~$200K/year incremental tax expense. Always model at 2.0–2.5% of purchase price for ongoing annual tax burden — NEVER use T-12 taxes in “Ours” column. Most common underwriting error in Texas acquisitions. Dallas Fed confirmed Texas banks modifying/extending loans above national average — sellers may have concealed problems through bank-facilitated deferrals.
CRITICAL
Tariff Renovation Cost Inflation
Steel/aluminum 50%, kitchen cabinets/vanities 25–50% (effective Jan 2026), lumber ~25% effective total. Add 15–25% contingency to all pre-2025 renovation budgets. Kitchen-focused renos most exposed — cabinets and vanities are the most tariff-impacted items in typical B+/A value-add scope. NAHB confirms $10,900/home added construction cost. Budget $10,000–$12,000/unit for mid-scope Houston/SA work.
HIGH
Broker T-12 / Pro Forma Manipulation
In a motivated-seller environment (bridge loan maturities, DSCR stress), standard manipulation patterns are elevated: GPR stated using market rent vs. in-place rent; occupancy cherry-picked from best snapshot month; payroll understated via shared-manager arrangements disappearing post-sale; tax reassessment risk not disclosed; bad debt normalized without documentation. Always underwrite from T-12 line items, never broker totals. Cross-check with on-site PM interview without broker present.
HIGH (deal level)
Iran War / Oil Price Spike
Active Iran conflict has spiked oil prices, complicating the Fed rate-cut path. For Houston: elevated oil is a net positive (energy sector employment). For all markets: higher-for-longer rates keep agency debt at 6.0–6.5% vs. declining to 5.5%. Stress-test exit refinance assumptions at 5.75% rather than 5.0%. JPMorgan: no cuts in 2026, possible hike in 2027 — 35% probability in market futures.
MEDIUM
Immigration Enforcement / Demand Reduction
Texas and Florida saw sharpest population growth pullbacks in 2025 partly from immigration enforcement (Yardi Feb 2026 report specifically cited). Class B/C workforce housing has higher undocumented renter exposure than Class A. Model Year 1 at flat demand in TX/FL workforce housing; avoid properties with documented HCV tenant concentration above 15% unless rents are supported by voucher contracts.
MEDIUM
Florida Hurricane / Insurance Volatility
2025 hurricane season was quiet, enabling insurance stabilization (Citizens −8.7%, 17 new private carriers). 2026 forecast: 7 hurricanes (3 intense) per Tropical Storm Risk. One Broward/Palm Beach direct hit reverses multi-year reform progress instantly. Budget $1,400–$1,800/unit/year; stress test at $2,200/unit/year for downside modeling. Average FL commercial MF premium: $8,292/yr (Insurify 2026).
MEDIUM
DOGE Federal Spending Cuts (San Antonio)
JBSA is the economic engine of San Antonio. Federal civilian employees at the base are a meaningful portion of the B+/A renter pool. DOGE targeting DoD civilian workforce could affect SA demand in ways not yet visible in current market data. Not a deal-breaker given the absolute scale and geopolitical necessity of the base, but monitor congressional budget closely.
LOW-MED
AI / Automation Renter Displacement (5-Year)
B+/A renters in TX and FL are disproportionately in professional services, healthcare admin, logistics, and call centers. Over a 5-year hold: healthcare (AI-resistant), military/government (AI-resistant), and skilled trades (AI-resistant) are most defensible. Technology and mid-level white-collar in tech-dependent submarkets face displacement risk. Prefer assets near healthcare/military/energy anchors specifically.
LOW (5yr)
National Recession (Mild/Moderate)
Moody's Analytics: ~25% recession probability for 2026. Freddie Mac downside: “If recession occurs while supply elevated, could have outsized impact on multifamily.” B+/A in military/healthcare-anchored markets most defensive. Bear scenario (11–12% IRR for Houston) represents mild recession stress. Markets with job diversity (SA military, FLL professional services) hold better. Avoid pure tech/speculation-driven markets in a recessionary scenario.
